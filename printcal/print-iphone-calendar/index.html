PrintCal: Print Your Calendar

How to print your iPhone calendar

Last updated: 29 October 2026

The short version The Calendar app on iPhone and iPad has no Print command. You can print the same calendars from a Mac, make do with a screenshot, or use an app on the phone that prints. Below, each way with its trade-offs, built-in options first.

Why there is no print button

Apple's Calendar app on iPhone and iPad can share an event, but it cannot print a month, a week or a list. The Google Calendar and Outlook apps on iPhone can't either. Your events are still there in iCloud (or in your Google or Outlook account), so the question is just which screen you print them from.

Option 1: Print from a Mac (built in, free)

If you have a Mac signed in to the same Apple Account, the Mac's Calendar app shows the same iCloud calendars, and it can print.

  1. On the Mac, open Calendar. Check that the calendars you want are ticked in the sidebar.
  2. Choose File → Print (or press ⌘P).
  3. In the dialog, pick a View (Day, Week, Month or List), the Paper size, the Time range, and which Calendars to include. Under Options you can turn on all-day events, timed events, mini months and a calendar key.
  4. Click Continue, then choose a printer or Save as PDF.

Good for: a quick month or week when a Mac is nearby. Watch for: on a full month the text gets very small and events are cut short; there is no large-print setting. Google or Outlook calendars appear only if they are added to the Mac too (System Settings → Internet Accounts).

Option 2: iCloud.com in a web browser

On any computer, sign in at icloud.com and open Calendar. There is no Print command of its own, but the browser's Print (⌘P or Ctrl+P) prints the month as it looks on screen.

Good for: a computer that isn't a Mac. Watch for: the result is a screen print: small text, events shortened, and sometimes the right-hand days cut off. Switch the browser to landscape and "Fit to page" before printing.

Option 3: A screenshot from the phone

  1. In Calendar, open the month view. On recent iOS versions, tap the view button at the top and choose Details (or Stacked) so the month shows event titles rather than dots.
  2. Take a screenshot (side button + volume up).
  3. Open it in Photos, tap Share, then Print, and choose your AirPrint printer.

Good for: a one-off reminder on the fridge. Watch for: it is a photo of the screen, so a tall phone shape on Letter paper, small text, and only the events that fit on the screen. Times and locations are not included.

Option 4: Print straight from the phone with PrintCal

PrintCal reads the calendars already on your iPhone or iPad (iCloud, Google, Outlook, Exchange, subscribed and shared) and lays them out as a proper page.

  1. Open PrintCal and allow calendar access. Choose Full Access; write-only access cannot read events. Access is read-only: the app never changes an event.
  2. The current month appears already laid out. Switch to Week or List if you prefer, and tick the calendars to include in the Calendars sheet.
  3. In Style, choose text size, bold or Large Print, which details to show (end times, location, notes), and Letter or A4.
  4. Tap Print to send the page to an AirPrint printer through the iPhone's print dialog, or Share to save a PDF to Files or iCloud Drive, or send it by Mail.

Good for: a readable month or week with every event on it, when there is no Mac around, or when the people reading it need big text. When a day is too full, the extra events go on an overflow page instead of being cut off. Watch for: like any app, it can only see calendars that are turned on in the Calendar app; a calendar hidden under Calendar → Calendars is hidden from PrintCal too. Month, week and list are free; day and year pages are part of the one-time Pro purchase.

Good to know

  • No printer? Every option above can save a PDF instead; print it later from a computer or a print shop.
  • Letter or A4. Pick the same paper size in the print dialog that the page was made for, or the printer will shrink or crop it.
  • Next year's calendar. Calendar on the Mac can print any date range. In PrintCal, the free version prints one month or one week at a time; printing a whole year, past or future, is part of Pro.
