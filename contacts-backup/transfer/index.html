Contacts Backup, Export, Merge

Transfer contacts to a new phone

Last updated: 22 October 2026

The short version Make a backup on the old phone, send the file to the new phone, open it there with the app, check the preview, and tap Restore. Nothing is uploaded to us at any step.

Before you start

  • Install the app on the old phone. That is where the backup is made.
  • Check the size of your address book. The free version backs up address books of up to 500 contacts. For example, a phone with 450 contacts is free; a phone with 800 contacts needs Pro on the old phone to make the backup. The app tells you before you start.
  • Restoring is always free, whatever the size, so the new phone never needs Pro just to bring contacts in.
  • Already synced? If all your contacts are in iCloud (iPhone to iPhone) or in your Google account (Android to Android), signing in on the new phone may bring them across on its own. A backup is still the safest way to make sure nothing is missed, and it is the only way across between iPhone and Android, or for contacts saved only "On My iPhone" or on the phone itself.

Step by step

  1. Back up the old phone. Open the app and tap Back Up Now. Wait for the report; it counts the phone numbers, emails and photos that were saved.
  2. Send the backup file to the new phone. Under Save to, pick the easiest way:
    • AirDrop: iPhone to iPhone, when both phones are next to each other.
    • Quick Share: Android to Android.
    • Files: save to iCloud Drive or Google Drive, then open it on the new phone. Works between iPhone and Android.
    • Mail: email it to yourself. Fine for small address books; a book with many photos may be too big to attach.
  3. Open the file on the new phone. Install the app on the new phone. Tap Restore from a backup (on Android, Restore), then Browse Files and pick the .vcf file. Or tap the file in AirDrop, Mail or Drive and choose the app.
  4. Check the preview. It shows how many contacts will be added and which ones already match. Untick anyone you don't want to bring.
  5. Pick where they go. Under Add them back to, choose the account that should keep them, usually iCloud on iPhone or your Google account on Android, so they also sync to your other devices.
  6. Tap Restore. The new phone's current contacts are backed up first, so you can undo from History if something looks wrong.

Examples

iPhone to iPhone

Maya has 320 contacts on her old iPhone, some saved only "On My iPhone". She taps Back Up Now, chooses AirDrop and sends the file to her new iPhone. On the new iPhone she taps the file, chooses the app, picks iCloud under Add them back to and taps Restore 320 contacts. All 320 are now in iCloud, including the ones that were never synced before.

iPhone to Android

Arjun is moving from an iPhone with 640 contacts to an Android phone. 640 is over the free limit, so he unlocks Pro on the iPhone and taps Back Up Now, then Files, and saves the file to Google Drive. On the Android phone he installs the app (restoring is free), taps Restore, Browse files, and picks the file from Drive. He chooses his Google account so the contacts also appear in Gmail, and taps Restore.

Android to iPhone

Lena's Android phone has 410 contacts. Quick Share doesn't reach an iPhone, so she chooses Files and saves the backup to Google Drive (or emails it to herself with Gmail). On the iPhone she opens the file from the Drive app or Mail, chooses the app, picks iCloud and restores.

Leaving a job: work contacts to your own account

Sam's work Exchange account holds 150 contacts that will disappear when the employer removes the account. In the app, Sam taps the accounts line on Home (or Settings, then Accounts and groups). The work account shows how many contacts exist only there, with a button to copy them to the personal account. Contacts already saved in both places are not copied twice. Do this before your last day.

Without the app on the new phone

A backup is an ordinary .vcf file, so the new phone can also import it directly. You won't get the preview or the duplicate check, so contacts that are already on the new phone may appear twice.

  • iPhone: tap the .vcf file in Files, Mail or Messages, then tap Add All Contacts.
  • Android: in the Contacts app, open Fix & manage and choose Import from file, then pick the .vcf file.

If you end up with duplicates, the app's Duplicates tab finds them and lets you merge them, with undo.

Good to know

  • Notes on iPhone: Apple doesn't let apps read contact notes, so a backup made on an iPhone doesn't include them. Backups made on Android do.
  • Keep the old phone until you've checked. Open a few contacts on the new phone, including some with photos, before you reset the old one.
  • Keep a copy. Save one backup to iCloud Drive, Google Drive or your computer. It is useful the next time you change phones too.
